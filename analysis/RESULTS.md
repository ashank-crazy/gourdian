# Validation results

Generated by `node analysis/validate.mjs` in 9 s. Every number below comes from
**synthetic** students simulated by `js/simulate.js`, where the true behavior is known. These results
show the methods work as intended on data that follows the model's assumptions. They are not evidence
about real students; that needs a pilot (see the DataNova write-up).

Setup: 200 students per scenario, 42 days of history each (about 59 sessions),
each with personal hazard coefficients drawn around a population mean (SD 0.35 on the log-odds scale).
Scenario A draws them around the app's starter prior. Scenario B draws them around a different "truth"
(no time-on-task effect, no night effect, a bigger relapse effect), so the prior is wrong.

## 4. Survival curves, recommendations and hourly rates

Each student's six-week Kaplan–Meier estimate against the same student's 1000-day long run.

| Minute | Students | Mean error | Mean abs. error | 95% band contains truth |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10 | 200 | 0.004 | 0.044 | 98% |
| 15 | 200 | 0.004 | 0.051 | 98% |
| 20 | 196 | -0.001 | 0.056 | 95% |

Recommended session length vs. the one the long run would give: identical for 62% of
students, within 5 minutes for 89% (n = 200).

Hourly slip rates (2739 student-hours): mean squared error 14.73 for raw rates,
1.80 with empirical-Bayes shrinkage, a **88% reduction**.

## 5. Policy simulation: fixed 25-minute sessions vs. the recommended length

200 students, four further weeks simulated twice with the same random numbers: once with every
session at 25 minutes, once at each student's recommended length (10 min: 96, 15 min: 47, 20 min: 19, 25 min: 12, 30 min: 10, 35 min: 2, 40 min: 2, 45 min: 1, 50 min: 11).

| | Fixed 25 min | Recommended length |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Slips per focus hour | 2.76 (2.47 to 3.04) | 2.12 (1.91 to 2.33) |
| Slip-free sessions | 0.45 (0.42 to 0.48) | 0.66 (0.64 to 0.68) |

Relative change in slips per focus hour: -0.099 (-0.164 to -0.034). This follows from the time-on-task effect
built into the simulator, so it shows the recommendation does what it should *if* that effect is real. Shorter
sessions also mean less focus time per session unless students run more of them.
